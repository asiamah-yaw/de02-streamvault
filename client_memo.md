## What I recommend

**Put the money into franchise bundles, provided the price per film stays within the audience lead.**

**Franchises:** **The Avengers** (1.79 billion per film, 4 films, weakest 79% of its series' typical); **The Dark Knight** (1.01 billion, 3 films, but Batman Begins only 37%: push on its price); **Pirates of the Caribbean** (962 million, 5 films, weakest 68%), effectively tied with **The Hobbit** (958 million, every film within 1%), so switch if Pirates is priced high.

**Standalones** (highest box office, all rated 8 or more): Inception, Interstellar, WALL·E, Forrest Gump (1994), Schindler's List (1993). If Finance rejects pre-2000 titles, use The Departed and Spirited Away.

**Talent:** only Christopher Nolan draws above typical across franchise and standalone films, but three of his films are already shortlisted, so he adds no independent evidence. Robert Downey Jr. is a lever for the Avengers deal only.

**Deal note: The Avengers** leads every franchise in both periods, 77% ahead of the next. From 2000, each film drew about **2.6 times** the typical other title on your list; its weakest still reached 79% of the series' typical. That 2.6 drove my view: if licence prices rise in step with audience size, paying up to 2.6 times a typical title's price, per film, is backed by demand. Above that, the advantage is paid away.

**What would change this:** bundle prices above the audience lead, or more post-2000 standalones (only 8 today) performing differently.
