## What I found

**Franchise films draw far bigger audiences than standalones, in both periods.** "Typical" means the middle film when ranked by box office.

| Period | Typical franchise film (US$) | Typical standalone (US$) | Franchise lead | Titles compared |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2000 onward** | 787 million | 283 million | **2.8 times** | 63 films in 24 series; 8 standalones |
| All years | 579 million | 162 million | 3.6 times | 96 films in 32 series; 16 standalones |

Each series counts once as evidence, and I required at least 5 on each side before naming a winner: from 2000 that is 24 series against 8 standalones.

The answer is the same in both periods. It holds with each side's biggest hit removed, and when the nine franchises with only one film on your list count as standalones (the lead narrows to 1.8 times from 2000). Middle entries are not weak: they earn about the same as the rest of their series.

**One caution that reverses the answer: audiences rate standalones higher (8.2 against 7.5 from 2000). If the goal were quality rather than audience size, standalones would win.**

## What I recommend

**Put the money into franchise bundles, provided the price per film stays within the audience lead.**

**Franchises** ("weakest" is the series' lowest-earning film, as a share of its typical film):

| Franchise | Typical film | Films | Weakest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Avengers** | 1.79 billion | 4 | 79% |
| **The Dark Knight** | 1.01 billion | 3 | 37% (Batman Begins): push on its price |
| **Pirates of the Caribbean** | 962 million | 5 | 68% |

**The Hobbit** (958 million, every film within 1% of its typical) is effectively tied with Pirates: switch if Pirates is priced high.

**Standalones** (highest box office, all rated 8 or more): Inception, Interstellar, WALL·E, Forrest Gump (1994), Schindler's List (1993). If Finance rejects pre-2000 titles, use The Departed and Spirited Away.

**Talent:** only Christopher Nolan draws above typical across franchise and standalone films, but three of his films are already shortlisted, so he adds no independent evidence. Robert Downey Jr. is a lever for the Avengers deal only.

**Deal note: The Avengers** leads every franchise in both periods, 77% ahead of the next. From 2000, each film drew about **2.6 times** the typical other title on your list; its weakest film still earned 79% of the series' typical film. That 2.6 drove my view: if licence prices rise in step with audience size, paying up to 2.6 times a typical title's price, per film, is backed by demand. Above that, the advantage is paid away.

**What would change this:** bundle prices above the audience lead, more post-2000 standalones (only 8 today) performing differently, or a decision to buy for audience ratings rather than audience size.
